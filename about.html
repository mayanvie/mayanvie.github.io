Why I'm making this switch

At the end of 2025, I left my job. The company moved to a location too far to commute to. The plan was simple: keep tutoring and teaching assistant work on the side, brush up my Chinese, and apply somewhere new.

The first keyword I typed into job boards was "content marketing," the role I'd defaulted to since graduation. I liked writing, liked creative content, and I'd actually worked in that role before.

But something felt off.

I didn't enjoy writing ad scripts, short-form content, or padding out long pieces for information that already existed everywhere online, though if the job required it, I'd still try to do it as well as I could. Then, picturing myself back at a desk doing that same work, I realized, honestly, I had no real motivation left to write a CV.

That's when I had no choice but to stop and actually think about what I wanted. I opened ChatGPT, dumped out a long list of my experience, interests, everything I'd done, and asked it to suggest roles that might fit.

Looking at that list, everything sounded decent, because I'm the type who likes a lot of things. So I asked myself something simpler: what's the one thing I've done for years and still get excited about?

Looking back at past jobs, the answer came into focus. I always lit up when I could take something long and confusing and turn it into something people could grasp quickly. And to me, how someone learns something was never just about the content, it's a psychology problem too.

I've also taught myself a lot, Chinese, English, so I know what it's like to figure things out alone, especially as an adult. Every time I cracked something new or solved a problem, I'd usually write it up (a handful of posts live on my Substack now). Alongside that, I've always gotten a specific kind of satisfaction from building systems, for work, for people, that run clean, tight, and easy to track. I couldn't tell you exactly where that comes from. I just know finishing one always feels deeply satisfying.

At first, Learning & Development sounded completely foreign to me. I had to look it up more than once, and when I brought it up with friends, almost no one had heard of it either. It took a while before I realized these were things I already liked doing, and had already done, I just hadn't called it that.

To be upfront: I don't have a formal L&D title, and I've never run an LMS at real company scale. I first tried closing that gap the structured way, building a whole Notion database to learn L&D theory from the ground up. A few weeks in, I realized it was too slow. So I changed strategy: build a case study first, learn whatever I'm missing while building it, then go deeper afterward, the same way I learn everything else. This portfolio grew out of that shift. It's not a list of what I know; it's evidence of what I've built, what I'm still learning, and where I'm headed.

I still think the fastest way to learn is by doing it for real. Right now I'm looking for an in-house L&D / Learning Ops role to build that depth at real scale, and I'm still open to short-term freelance projects, hoping the right people can find me.
